How it works

  1. Drop in your take

    Record yourself talking on your phone or camera, retakes and all, and drop the file into Reel Studio.

  2. It edits the reel

    It listens, keeps your best take of every line, cuts the dead air, and adds captions, b-roll, your music and a cover.

  3. Review in plain English

    Type notes like "music too loud at the start", or attach a picture to the words it belongs on. Then export, or send it to your phone.

What you get

  • Your best take of every line, picked for you
  • Pauses, flubs and false starts cut out
  • Captions timed to your words, with measured sync
  • B-roll pictures and text cards on the words they belong to
  • Your own music mixed under your voice
  • A cover frame, and a post caption with hashtags
  • Review notes in plain English, with pictures, GIFs or clips attached
  • Every change saved as a new version, side by side with the last
  • "Edit it myself" tabs for the cut, subtitles, music, pictures and cover
  • Send to Phone over your Wi-Fi
  • Everything renders on your computer; your videos are not uploaded
  • Open source under the MIT license

Questions

Is it really open source?

Yes. All of Reel Studio's code is on GitHub under the MIT license. You can read it, build it yourself, change it and share it. Every release on GitHub also publishes the source of the bundled programs next to the installers.

Then why pay?

Paying is how the app gets made. It funds development, and it gets you the signed, ready-to-run installers, the updates, and email help when something goes wrong. The downloads are public and we don't check who paid, so it's on the honor system. Thanks for paying for this.

Do I need an account?

Not with us. There is no Reel Studio account and no sign-up. For the AI editor you use your own Claude or ChatGPT plan: you sign in to Anthropic's or OpenAI's own app with their own sign-in page, and Reel Studio never sees that login.

What does it send over the network?

No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reports. Transcription, cutting, captions and rendering all run on your computer. The app goes online only for these:

  • Update check: at most once a day, a request to GitHub's public releases list. You can turn it off in Settings.
  • First run: a one-time download of the speech-recognition model from Hugging Face.
  • Pictures and icons, when the editor looks for them: searches on Openverse, Wikimedia Commons, Wikidata, Wikipedia, TVmaze and jsDelivr (Simple Icons).
  • Links: a picture, video or song from a link you paste, or a screenshot of a web page the editor names in your reel.
  • Your AI, when you use the editor: your Claude Code or Codex app sends the transcript, the project settings, a few still frames and your notes to Anthropic or OpenAI. If you paste your own API key instead, that goes to the Anthropic API or the server you set up.
  • Setup, only if you click install: the official Claude Code or Codex installer from Anthropic or OpenAI.

Full details are in the privacy policy.

Does it work without an AI?

Partly. Without an AI connected it still transcribes, cuts the pauses and repeated starts, adds captions, mixes in your song and makes a cover. Picking takes, finding pictures, writing the post caption and handling your notes need the AI editor.

Windows says "Windows protected your PC". Is that safe?

That blue box appears for programs from small, new publishers that don't have Microsoft's paid "known publisher" stamp yet, and the Windows installer doesn't have one. Click More info, then Run anyway. You only do it once. Download the installer only from the official GitHub releases page. The Mac app is signed and notarized by Apple, so it opens with a double-click.

Which Macs and PCs does it run on?

Macs with an Apple chip (M1 or newer) on macOS 14 Sonoma or newer, and 64-bit Windows 10 or 11. Intel Macs are not supported yet. You need about 2 GB of free space and an internet connection the first time you open it.
